# Flow: Portfolio / Personal Brand

Read this after intent is identified as portfolio / personal brand. It overrides and extends `interview/INTERVIEW.md`'s generic questions — ask these on top, not instead.

## Ask these, on top of the generic 5

1. **What do you want visitors to do?** Hire you, follow you, book you, or explore the work.
   This decides the CTA and the whole page structure — a hire-me page and a follow-me page aren't built the same way.
2. **What's your 3-5 best pieces of work?**
   Curated beats comprehensive. Ask for the best, not the full archive.
3. **What's your niche or specialty?**
   Generalists get generic pages. Push for specificity — it's the differentiator here more than anywhere else.
4. **What's your personality or voice?**
   Portfolio pages live or die on personality. Sterile reads as forgettable.

## Section Template

1. Hero: name + what you do + one line that shows personality
2. Best work showcase: 3-5 pieces, each with problem → solution → outcome
3. About / story: who you are, how you got here, what drives you
4. Skills or specialties: brief — not a skills matrix
5. Client list or testimonials: real names, real companies, what they said
6. Contact / hire CTA: a clear next step

## Copy Direction

- The work IS the page. Let the projects speak; minimize copy around them.
- Personality has to come through — a generic portfolio is a forgotten portfolio.
- "View my work" is not a CTA. "Hire me for your next project" is.
- 3-5 projects beats 30 — curation signals confidence, not a thin portfolio.
- The about section is the most-read section on portfolio pages. It's where this is won or lost — don't phone it in.

## Edge Cases

- Developer portfolio → code samples, GitHub links, live demos can replace visual work entirely.
- Writer portfolio → published pieces, publication names, writing samples in place of "projects."
- Speaker / consultant → speaking videos, event photos, a booking CTA instead of "hire me."
- Multi-discipline → pick ONE primary focus for the main page; link out to separate sections for the rest. Don't split the hero three ways.
