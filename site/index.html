You answer five questions. Your agent does the rest in order.

  1. Ask

    Before any code, your agent asks what you sell, who it's for, where visitors come from, and why you instead of the alternatives. One or two questions at a time. Your answers become a brief you approve.

  2. Commit

    The agent writes copy section by section, then commits to one visual direction and one idea a competitor's page couldn't reuse. No concept you've approved, no code.

  3. Check

    62 numbered checks run before you see the page. Em dashes, banned phrases, contrast and boxed layouts get fixed automatically. Anything that needs your judgment comes back as a short list.

In a hurry? Say "skip the questions." The agent states its assumptions and builds.

Rules your agent can't talk its way around.

Zero em dashes.

Not a budget of one. The em dash is the most cited tell of AI copy, so Deft removes every one, including from the title tag and alt text.

One direction, exact colors.

Six named visual directions, each with real palette values. The agent picks one and commits. An unmodified Tailwind ramp fails check V-25.

Nothing invented.

No testimonials nobody said, no logos you don't have, no "trusted by thousands." When proof is thin, the page claims less. An honest modest page beats an impressive suspicious one.

The Template Test.

Swap your name and logo for a competitor's. If the page still works, it has no idea, and it goes back to the concept stage (check V-21).

Questions people ask before starring.

Isn't this just a long prompt?
A prompt asks nicely. Deft gives the agent stages it can't skip, a concept it has to get approved, and 62 numbered checks the page has to pass. A failed V-21 sends the page back to the concept stage, not to a quick patch.
Does it work with my agent?
If your agent reads a project instructions file, yes. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Windsurf all do.
Will the interview slow me down?
Five core questions, one or two at a time. If you'd rather skip them, say so. The agent lists the assumptions it's making about your product, audience and tone, and you correct what's wrong.
What does it build in?
Deft decides what the page says and how it looks. Your agent builds it in whatever your project already uses.
Can I still have a purple gradient?
Yes. Your agent will mention the trade-off once, sigh quietly, and build it.
How do I uninstall it?
Delete the folder. It's Markdown files, nothing else.
